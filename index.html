01

Targeted LNPs · Lymphatic endothelium

Discover an unexpected route into lymphatic endothelium

  1. HYPOTHESIS

    A possible route into lymphatic endothelium, inspired by liver uptake.

  2. CELL EVIDENCE

    Demonstrated in cultured lymphatic endothelial cells.

  3. IN VIVO EVIDENCE

    After subcutaneous delivery of optimized siRNA-LNPs in a preclinical model.

MY CONTRIBUTIONConceived and experimentally validated the ApoE-mediated lymphatic delivery concept with collaborators; first author of the study.

VALUE FOR R&DGenerate and test targeting hypotheses for cell populations that are difficult to reach.

J Control Release · 2023 · PubMed ↗Co-inventor · lymphatic LNP application ↘
Approach & supporting studies+

The problem

LNP uptake through ApoE is commonly associated with liver delivery. The challenge was to deliver siRNA to lymphatic endothelial cells.

How the study tested it

The ApoE–LDLR hypothesis guided tests of ApoE-dependent LNP uptake. Subcutaneous delivery and a dense coating of short-chain PEG lipids then enabled evaluation in vivo.

Research approach

Generate an unexpected mechanistic hypothesis, test it, and turn the finding into a strategy for extrahepatic delivery.

Another targeting approach

A complementary approach used CLIP chemistry to attach targeting antibodies to LNPs. Anti-podoplanin LNPs demonstrated lymphatic endothelial delivery in vitro and in vivo.

02

Tumor endothelium · Extracellular matrix

Change the tissue to improve particle delivery

The different idea

Test whether vascular normalization could improve delivery of 100 nm particles—even though tumor accumulation is often attributed to leaky vessels.

Experimental result

Particle distribution and treatment efficacy improved despite vascular maturation in the preclinical study. Inhibiting matrix metalloproteinases or depleting macrophages removed the distribution benefit.

MY CONTRIBUTIONDeveloped the hypothesis that vascular normalization could improve particle delivery, and investigated the underlying mechanism; first author of the matrix-remodeling study.

VALUE FOR R&DExpand the design choices for delivery by addressing tissue biology as well as particle composition.

Mol Ther · 2016 · PubMed ↗
Approach & supporting studies+

The problem

The tissue environment can limit how nanoparticles distribute inside a tumor, even after particles reach its blood vessels.

How the study tested it

Tumor endothelial cell-targeted liposomal siRNA inhibited VEGFR2. The study examined vascular maturation, intratumoral particle distribution and mechanisms involving matrix remodeling.

Research approach

Investigate a biological barrier as a modifiable part of the delivery system, and use mechanistic experiments to test the explanation.

03

Lymphatic imaging · Design of Experiments

Design for the first lymph node

The different idea

Combine transport, macrophage uptake and the injection environment in the formulation strategy, then optimize the lipid composition systematically.

Experimental result

The studies reported improved sentinel-node detection with hyaluronidase and greater sentinel-node selectivity of the optimized particles than indocyanine green in preclinical models.

MY CONTRIBUTIONCo-first and co-corresponding author of the DoE study; contributed to conceptualization, experiments, data curation, visualization and writing.

VALUE FOR R&DTranslate a biological delivery objective into formulation criteria and informative experiments.

Pharmaceutics · 2021 · PubMed ↗
Approach & supporting studies+

The problem

A sentinel-node probe needs to accumulate in the first draining node with limited spread to downstream nodes.

How the study tested it

A five-factor, two-level design tested 16 conditions covering phospholipid, sterol, PEG-lipid content, filter size and hyaluronidase. Follow-up experiments varied phosphatidylserine content and compared sentinel-node imaging with indocyanine green.

Research approach

Define the biological performance objective, study its determinants, and use DoE to guide formulation choices.

Measured result & comparison

In a follow-up mouse experiment, 30% phosphatidylserine liposomes gave 2.8-fold greater sentinel-node selectivity than PS-free liposomes. Selectivity was the sentinel-to-axillary-node accumulation ratio (n = 3 per condition; Figure 3).
